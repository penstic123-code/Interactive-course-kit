The core loop behind "one last game" at 1 a.m.

The end of one game quietly hands you a reason to start the next. You nearly won. The character you just unlocked is still untried. Draw "the end leads back to the start" and you get a circle. The chain of things a player repeats most often is called the core loop.

Core loop

核心循环

A common game design term with no single inventor

The few things a player does over and over.

The chain of actions a player repeats in a game, which ends back at the start, ready for another round.

Pac-Man: eat dots, dodge ghosts, grab a power pellet, chase the ghosts, clear the maze (see figure).

Three loops, one inside the other

Every second you jump, hit and dodge. Every few minutes you finish a match. Every few days you have saved enough to buy new gear.

Pac-Man: second by second you dodge ghosts, minute by minute you clear a maze, and there is almost no long-term growth.

Daily phone game: the tapping is thin, but the daily log-in and levelling up are thick.

Counterexample: you log in every day to collect a prize, but there is nothing to actually do. The inner loop is empty, so the outer rewards are just paying you to show up.

From the button to the prize

A surprise reward hits harder than an expected one

Dopamine neurons in the brain respond less to the reward itself than to how much better it was than expected. Once a reward is fully predicted, the response almost disappears. (Simplified.)

Source: Schultz and colleagues, 1997, Science. Check it: the guessing game below.

How random is "random" here?A bit of dice maths you can skip

If each try has a 1 in 5 chance of a prize, you win once every five tries on average. Yet the chance of ten misses in a row is still about 11%.

Have a guess first

Both schemes give a prize once every five tries on average. Which one is harder to stop?

  • Exactly every fifth tryOnce you know the pattern, you slack off right after each prize.
  • A different number each timeEvery try might be the one.
  • No real differenceThere is a big difference.
The unpredictable scheme is the sticky one. It is how loot boxes and slot machines pay out.

After the food stops, how many more times will the pigeon peck?
